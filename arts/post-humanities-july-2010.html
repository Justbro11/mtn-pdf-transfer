[1] Latifa became a disabled person due to a serious illness that caused her paralysis in her early childhood. When she was nineteen years old, she lived in a remote village in the Rif Mountains. As one of six children in a poor family of a labourer, she got married at the age of 16. At that time, she was hardly able to walk by herself. However, this marriage could not last for long. Very soon, Latifa's husband and his parents abandoned her, blaming her for her physical handicap. Later, her husband divorced her and got married again. With a sense of rejection and despair, she went back to her parents' home.

[2] When her village was selected for a women literacy project, Latifa got in contact with the teachers in this project. When they talked to her and to some women in the village, she immediately joined literacy classes. This changed her life completely. Guidance, care, and affection of the teachers helped build her personality. She emerged as a leader of girls, helping them to study and come out of their isolation for a new life.

[3] Later on, Latifa, who had to use a wheelchair to move, joined a programme organised by the Ministry of Youth and Sport for the benefit of the disabled. She learnt traditional embroidery in a two- month training programme, and began earning money from making tablecloths and napkins. Latifa became the breadwinner in her family.

[4] Latifa took the initiative and set up an association of young boys and girls of her village. When drought hit the village and water became scarce, she wrote letters to German non-governmental organizations asking them for help. She cannot forget the day when a caravan of cars and trucks arrived in the village. She was cheered by the villagers when water started to pour out of a fountain designed by a German engineer.

[5] One could see Latifa in her struggle for social justice, leading a group of young people with a sense of confidence in her voice and gestures. She stood up against arranged marriages in rural areas around the village and persuaded parents to send their children to school. She still walks with difficulty, but the confidence of her voice helps her overcome her physical handicap. For her, life has just begun. From a lonely, fragile figure, Latifa is now a good educator and a role model. When people ask her how her life has changed, she points with a smile of gratitude to her old classroom in the farthest corner of her village.

Writing

Writing: Describe an end-of-year school party. (10 pts)

An Unforgettable End-of-Year Party

Last Friday, our school organised a party to celebrate the end of the school year. It took place in the school yard in the afternoon, and the whole school took part: students, teachers and even some parents.

There were many activities. Some students sang songs and performed sketches, others played music and danced. There was also a football match between students and teachers, and the headmaster gave prizes to the best students. We ate delicious cakes and drank juice together.

I felt very happy and a little sad, because I will miss my friends and teachers during the holidays. It was a wonderful day that I will never forget.

Exam Tips and Key Vocabulary

What this exam tests

The reading text of the July 2010 Humanities exam is about Latifa, a disabled woman who became a leader in her village. The paper tests Reading, Language and Writing, so read the text twice before answering and base every answer on the text.

Key vocabulary from the text

  • disabled: ذو إعاقة
  • paralysis: شلل
  • abandon: يتخلى عن
  • despair: يأس
  • breadwinner: المعيل
  • drought: جفاف
  • scarce: نادر
  • role model: قدوة
  • gratitude: امتنان
  • struggle: نضال

Grammar points in this exam

  • Word formation (poverty, illiterate)
  • Collocations (higher education, take place)
  • Third conditional, reported speech and passive
  • Past perfect and first conditional
  • Relative pronouns, language functions and linking words

Revise these structures: the Language section of this exam is built on them.

Writing tips

The writing task asks for an article describing an end-of-year school party. Say when it took place, who participated, what activities there were and how you felt.
