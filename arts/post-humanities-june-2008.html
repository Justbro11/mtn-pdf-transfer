[1] Iman Soufiani is a young inventor. Since she was a little girl, she had many ideas to put into practice. But she never had the chance to do so. As she grew up, she did not give up her natural curiosity towards the world. With the establishment of scientific clubs in public schools, she finally had the chance to try out creative ideas and transform them into remarkable inventions.

[2] When she was 16, she invented a new kind of glue out of simple materials. "I tried the new glue on iron, wood, glass, and carpet; it worked wonderfully," Iman says.

[3] The new glue is made of cork and benzene. "The idea is originally not mine," Iman says. "I talked to a relative a man who is a college graduate. She told me that in the past our grandparents used cork and benzene to glue things together," she adds. "I liked the idea and reported my desire to experiment with it to my teacher in the scientific club in my school. In the laboratory, Mrs Madbouli helped me out in mixing the two materials together. She was afraid that there would be a chemical reaction that might hurt me," Iman explains.

[4] One good benefit of the new glue is that it costs almost nothing. Moreover, it is environment-friendly. She hopes that a businessman would sponsor the idea one day and build a factory that produces this glue.

[5] Iman is 17 years old now and is in the eleventh grade. She is in the scientific section. "I chose it because I like Science and Mathematics; and I dislike History," Iman says. Her grades fall between 100 and 95 out of 100, with an exception to English. "My grade is acceptable but it is not that good, for that I signed up for an English course last summer," says Iman.

[6] Iman believes that everyone is gifted in something. And for anyone who feels talented, she advises them to work hard, not to give up and to share their talents with their friends and anybody who might help them.

Writing: Tell the story of someone who emigrated. (10 pts)

One of your friends, relatives, or neighbours graduated from a high institution and got a diploma (doctor, engineer, etc). He/she then emigrated to another country to work. Write about his/her story. (Approximately 200 words)
– Who the person is
– Type and place of study and diploma
– Where he/she emigrated
– The reasons of emigration
– Positive and/or negative effects of emigration on his/her life in that country
– His/her feeling, opinion and attitudes

Writing

Writing: Tell the story of someone who emigrated. (10 pts)

My cousin Youssef is thirty years old. He studied engineering at the National School of Applied Sciences in Casablanca and got his diploma in 2018. After graduation, he looked for a job in Morocco for almost a year, but he could not find one that matched his skills.

Then he emigrated to Canada, where a company offered him a good contract. He left mainly because of unemployment and low salaries. In Canada, he earns a high salary and works with modern technology. However, he misses his family and friends, and he sometimes feels lonely.
In my opinion, emigration is a personal choice, but Morocco loses its skilled young people. I hope he will come back one day and help develop his country.

Exam Tips and Key Vocabulary

What this exam tests

The reading text of the June 2008 Humanities exam is about Iman Soufiani, a young inventor who made a new kind of glue. The paper tests Reading, Language and Writing, so read the text twice before answering and base every answer on the text.

Key vocabulary from the text

  • inventor: مخترع
  • invention: اختراع
  • glue: غراء
  • cork: فلين
  • sponsor: يرعى / يموّل
  • gifted: موهوب
  • talent: موهبة
  • environment-friendly: صديق للبيئة
  • brain drain: هجرة الأدمغة
  • emigration: الهجرة

Grammar points in this exam

  • Collocations (critical thinking, make fun of)
  • Word formation (literacy, tolerate, growth)
  • Tenses and the past perfect
  • Passive voice and reported speech
  • Linking words and language functions

Revise these structures: the Language section of this exam is built on them.

Writing tips

The writing task asks you to tell the story of someone who studied and then emigrated. Follow the guiding points: who, what and where he/she studied, where and why he/she emigrated, the effects, and your feelings.
