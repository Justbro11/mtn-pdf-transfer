[1] When computers first started to be used on a wide scale, some people predicted that we would spend so long staring at computer screens that we would end up forgetting how to talk to one another. But in fact, the rapid expansion of electronic communication in the 21st century has had the opposite effect. Rather than retreating into themselves, people are using new technology, in particular email and text messaging, to find more and more ways to expand their network of friends.

[2] Jane Adams, 23, sends so many messages to her friends. She’s known as the Text Queen. ‘My friends and I take our phones out with us and send messages to other friends saying “we’re in this club and it’s really good. Come and meet us”, she said. It means we don’t have to spend ages planning an evening out. You can just send the same message to everyone.

[3] Text messaging and email also help Jane keep in touch with old schoolmates she would probably have lost contact with otherwise. She finds that it’s easier to send a message saying ''Hi! thinking of you'', rather than having to write a long letter.

[4] It seems these forms of communication have filled a gap, offering something that face-to-face conversation does not. Professor Pam Briggs, a psychologist at the University of Northumbria, believes they have become popular because they give people an opportunity to present themselves in the way that they want to. ‘People seem to really enjoy sending text messages and emails, she says. ‘They can take their time planning their message. They prefer it to speaking on the phone to each other- maybe also because this way they can choose when they want to respond to someone.’

[5] The fact that text messages are so quick and easy is a big part of the attraction. Many people also find text messaging more informal than making a phone call or writing a letter, and therefore simpler to use. Ann Rose, who teaches at a London school, uses email and text messaging to keep in touch with students. ‘I have always given my number out to students and told them to call me if they have any problems. But no one ever did. Now, they often email or text me with questions about their work. They don’t find it difficult to keep in touch that way, whereas they might feel that a phone call is more of an interruption, ‘she said.

[6] So is all good, Ann has identified one negative result of text messaging. ‘The popularity of this way of writing among my students,’ she says ‘can cause a few difficulties as they have started using abbreviations such as ‘ruok’ for ‘are you okay? And ‘thx’ for thanks”

Writing

Writing: Write a short article for the school magazine. (10 pts)

What School Has Given Me

School is much more than a building where we learn lessons: it shapes our character and our future. I would like to share my experience as a student and the benefits I get from education.

My favorite subjects are English and science. In English I learn to communicate with people from other countries, and in science I learn to think logically and solve problems. School also teaches me teamwork, because we prepare projects and take part in clubs together. My teachers and my classmates have become a second family, and they support me when I am under pressure.

In the future, I would like to carry on my studies at university and become an engineer. Education will give me the skills to find a good job and to help my family, who have always sacrificed for my studies.

To sum up, education changes the life of the student and the life of his or her family. That is why every young person should value school.

Exam Tips and Key Vocabulary

What this exam tests

The reading text of the July 2008 Arts exam is about how email and text messaging help people stay in touch, with Jane Adams, known as the Text Queen. The paper tests Reading, Language and Writing, so read the text twice before answering and base every answer on the text.

Key vocabulary from the text

  • expand: يوسع
  • network: شبكة
  • opportunity: فرصة
  • informal: غير رسمي
  • abbreviation: اختصار
  • attraction: جاذبية
  • interruption: مقاطعة
  • retreat: ينسحب
  • keep in touch: يبقى على اتصال
  • face-to-face: وجها لوجه

Grammar points in this exam

  • Relative pronouns (whose, who, which)
  • Passive voice and reported commands
  • Wishes: If only + past simple
  • Past perfect continuous and future perfect
  • Phrasal verbs (look forward to, turn down, make up)
  • Linking words (although, therefore, so as not to)

Revise these structures: the Language section of this exam is built on them.

Writing tips

The writing task asks for a short article for the school magazine. Give it a title, introduce the topic, then cover each suggestion (subjects, school life, future projects, effect on your family) in a short paragraph. Use linking words and finish with a short conclusion.
