[1] I was walking around on a Saturday afternoon. It was cold and it was starting to rain. I was looking for a restaurant or a café where I could sit and have something to drink. When I saw one, I crossed the street and I was there. I sat at a round table and asked for a cup of coffee. While I was waiting for my order, I realized that there were other people in the place, but I sensed loneliness. I saw their bodies, but I couldn't feel their souls. That was because their souls didn't belong to them; instead they belonged to the Internet.

[2] I stood up and walked between the tables. When I came to the biggest computer, I saw a thin, small man sitting in front of it. "I'm Steve", he finally answered after I had asked him a couple of times what his name was. "I can't talk with you. I'm busy," he said. I thought he was working, and I apologized. He didn't say anything. Before going back to my seat, I wanted to see what he was doing. I stood behind him. He was not working. He was chatting online with somebody - probably someone he didn't know--and, at the same time, he was playing a computer game - a war game. I was astonished.

[3] Why didn't Steve want to talk to me? I tried again to communicate with that computer geek, but not a word came out of his mouth. I touched his shoulder, but there was no reaction. I was getting upset. I put my hand in front of the monitor, and he started shouting, "leave me alone!" I took a few steps back. I was ashamed. I wanted to look and see if all those people in the café were looking at me, so I raised my head, but there was nobody interested in what was happening. I walked back to my table, and I noticed everybody was using a computer.

[4] At that moment, I realized that the people in that place were having a cup of coffee and a nice conversation with their machines, not with people. All of them were more interested in having a relationship with the computer, particularly Steve. I wouldn't want to imagine the future of human beings if they preferred sharing their lives with machines instead of people. I was worried and I sank in my thoughts. I didn't even realize that the coffee was bad, just as Steve didn't even realize that there was a person next to him.

Writing: Write an email describing the best place you have visited. (10 pts)

Dear Hamza,

How are you? I am writing to tell you about the best place I have ever visited. Last summer, my family and I spent a week in Chefchaouen, a small city in the north of Morocco.

The city is very attractive. All the houses are painted in blue and white, and the streets are narrow and traditional. The weather was nice and the people were friendly. We visited the old medina, walked in the mountains and tasted delicious local food.

I felt happy and relaxed, and it was an unforgettable holiday. I hope we can go there together one day.

Write to me soon.
Your friend,
Karim

Exam Tips and Key Vocabulary

What this exam tests

The reading text of the June 2010 Humanities exam is about Steve and computer addiction in a café. The paper tests Reading, Language and Writing, so read the text twice before answering and base every answer on the text.

Key vocabulary from the text

  • addiction: إدمان
  • astonished: مندهش
  • monitor: شاشة الحاسوب
  • geek: مهووس بالحواسيب
  • loneliness: وحدة
  • ashamed: خجلان
  • shoulder: كتف
  • machine: آلة
  • relationship: علاقة
  • realize: يدرك

Grammar points in this exam

  • Phrasal verbs (fill in, apply for, set up, come back)
  • Gerund and past perfect
  • Linking words (because, despite)
  • If only, reported advice, passive and conditionals
  • Word formation (citizenship, involvement) and language functions

Revise these structures: the Language section of this exam is built on them.

Writing tips

The writing task asks for an email to a friend about the best place you have visited. Greet your friend, name the place, say when you went, describe it and say how you felt.
