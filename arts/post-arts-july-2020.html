[1] More and more young people are taking on voluntary work nowadays. Some even travel abroad and do unpaid work for charity or non-profit organizations there. They help to build homes for poor families, teach children, take care of orphans, or help to protect endangered species. There is a wide variety of voluntary jobs to choose from.

[2] A number of charity groups organize educational programmes for children around the world. Many students volunteer for these types of projects. They teach subjects like English and Mathematics to children while staying in their community and getting to know the people and their culture. It is also a great opportunity for them to work and train with local teachers and develop their own skills. It is also a learning experience for both the children and the teacher. “I had just graduated from high school when I volunteered as a teacher in Uganda,” said James, a British volunteer. “Leading a class was a challenging experience for me. I was just a teenager and I had no experience of teaching before.”

[3] Young people who would like to help in a very practical way can participate in one of the many construction projects. Several charity organizations help build houses or schools for poor children around the world. In the village of Kamakwie in Sierra Leone, for example, a team of young volunteers built a secondary school for poor orphans. Beth had a similar experience. She said: “My first volunteering abroad was organized by my school. We went to Swaziland for three weeks and a half. It was the first time I had the opportunity to do something important; spending 10 days working in a rural community building a school kitchen for a woman who would cook for the local children.”

[4] For people interested in wildlife, conservation groups organize volunteer holidays throughout the world. They can help wildlife research in countries like Kenya and Botswana or protect turtles in Mexico, Zanzibar, or elsewhere. On Redang Island in Malaysia, volunteers come from all over the world to help conserve the endangered green turtle. Participants monitor the turtles that come onto the beach and protect them and their eggs. They also get to enjoy the beach and even swim with the turtles sometimes.

[5] Volunteers have to be realistic though and understand that they cannot change the world in two or three weeks. To some volunteers, it seems that they are not doing much by helping out for only a couple of weeks. However, thanks to young volunteers, the lives of millions of people around the world are improving. Adapted from :https://znanija.com

Writing

Task 1: Complete the following paragraph. (4 pts)

There are many benefits of studying abroad. To start with, students can learn a foreign language faster because they use it every day. In addition, they discover new cultures and make friends from different countries. What’s more, they get a high-quality education that helps them find better jobs. Last but not least, they become more independent and responsible.

Task 2: Write an article about posting videos on social media. (6 pts)

Why Do Young People Post Videos Online?

More and more young people are posting videos on YouTube and TikTok. There are several reasons for this.

First, they want to express themselves and show their talents, such as singing, dancing or cooking. Second, some of them hope to become famous and to earn money from their videos. Moreover, posting videos is an easy way to make new friends and to share ideas with people from all over the world. Finally, many teenagers do it simply for fun.

In conclusion, posting videos can be useful, but young people should be careful about their privacy and about what they publish.

Exam Tips and Key Vocabulary

What this exam tests

The reading text of the July 2020 Arts exam is about young people who do voluntary work at home and abroad. The paper tests Reading, Language and Writing, so read the text twice before answering and base every answer on the text.

Key vocabulary from the text

  • voluntary work: عمل تطوعي
  • charity: جمعية خيرية
  • non-profit: غير ربحي
  • orphan: يتيم
  • endangered: مهدد بالانقراض
  • construction: بناء
  • conservation: حماية البيئة
  • realistic: واقعي
  • opportunity: فرصة
  • volunteer: متطوع

Grammar points in this exam

  • Modals (must) and relative pronoun which
  • Collocations (financial difficulties, local customs)
  • Word formation (participation, competitive)
  • Past perfect and future perfect
  • Passive, reported speech and although
  • Language functions

Revise these structures: the Language section of this exam is built on them.

Writing tips

The writing paper has two tasks. In task 1, complete the paragraph about the benefits of studying abroad keeping the linking words. In task 2, write an article on why young people post videos on social media.
