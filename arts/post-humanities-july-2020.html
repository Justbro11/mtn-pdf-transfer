[1] The Brazilian journalist Sebastião Salgado and his wife Lélia decided to show that dedicated people can make a change. They were able to completely restore an entire forest that had been devastated by human action in their home town Minas Gerais in Brazil.

[2] Upon returning to his homeland from a duty as a journalist in Rwanda, Salgado felt very sad when he found the forest was lost and the animals had gone. It was not the tropical paradise that he remembered as a child. Then his wife came up with the fabulous idea of replanting the forest, something which they started later in 1994. Salgado said: "The land was as sick as I was; everything was destroyed. Only about 0.5% of the land was covered with trees. And when we began to do that, all the insects, birds and fish returned. This was the most important moment."

[3] To make their dream come true, Salgado and his wife recruited partners to plant trees. They also raised funds for their project. Also, in April 1998, they founded the Instituto Terra, a small organization that has since planted 4 million trees and has brought the forest back to life. Through the Instituto Terra, the couple managed to replant 2.7 million trees over two decades. They have completely transformed the environment back into its original condition. An entire ecosystem was built again thanks to this organisation. Now, the forest is thriving with animals and wildlife: 172 bird species, 33 species of animals, 293 species of plants and 15 species of reptiles have returned.

[4] The process has provided Salgado with one of the answers to climate change. He believes that "we have a solution. We need to replant the forest. You need forests with native trees if you want the serpents and the other insects to come back. And if you plant forests that don't belong to the area, the animals don't come there and the forest is silent."

[5] The reforestation effort of Sebastião and Lélia is incredible, but it will only last if people learn to respect the importance of the ecosystems. That's why the couple started the Center for Environmental Education and Recovery (CERA) in 2012. The aim is to educate farmers, teachers, businesses and government officials about environmental recovery and conservation techniques. The hope is that they will inspire those working near the forest to adopt more sustainable practices. Many organizations and individual volunteers are doing their best to protect the forest worldwide. The world needs more individual support and involvement to save the planet.

Exam Tips and Key Vocabulary

What this exam tests

The reading text of the July 2020 Humanities exam is about the Salgado couple who replanted a whole forest in Brazil. The paper tests Reading, Language and Writing, so read the text twice before answering and base every answer on the text.

Key vocabulary from the text

  • restore: يعيد / يرمم
  • devastated: مدمَّر
  • recruit: يستقطب
  • raise funds: يجمع التبرعات
  • ecosystem: نظام بيئي
  • thrive: يزدهر
  • native: محلي
  • reforestation: إعادة التشجير
  • conservation: حماية / حفظ
  • wildlife: حياة برية

Grammar points in this exam

  • Collocations (computer technologies, local customs)
  • Word formation (cultural, responsibility)
  • Modals (shouldn't) and relative pronouns
  • Tenses (past perfect, future perfect)
  • Reported speech, passive voice, cause (due to) and language functions

Revise these structures: the Language section of this exam is built on them.

Writing tips

The writing paper has two tasks. In task 1, complete the paragraph about the advantages of sports. In task 2, write an article for your school magazine encouraging students to join school activities and clubs.
