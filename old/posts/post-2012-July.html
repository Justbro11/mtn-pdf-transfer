[1] Saadia is 39 years old. She has lived in a poor area in Oued Zem with her husband for the past 20 years and now has five children. She started her professional career in Casablanca, working in a factory during the day and weaving carpets at home in the evening. However, after the birth of her first child, she had to quit her job in the factory to take care of her family.

[2] “I was working day and night to produce goods to sell around Casablanca at very low prices. I know that my products were worth more but I had no other option, just like all the other women from our area working for shoe and shirt makers,” Saadia said.

[3] This experience convinced Saadia of the importance of women’s contribution to family budgets. “When a woman brings money into the household, it changes the way she is looked at. Women are more respected for taking part in the household finances and can then take more decisions within the family,” she says.

[4] Saadia met members of the Near East Foundation in 2006 and enthusiastically took the opportunity to encourage women in her area to create an artisans’ cooperative. She was quickly chosen for the presidency of the cooperative given her profile, her capacity, and leadership abilities. Despite her family obligations, she has taken modern and traditional sewing lessons that she is currently teaching to other members of the cooperative. She has provided assistance to all the training workshops organized by local authorities, focusing on administrative and financial management.

[5] Saadia is now strongly convinced of the success of the cooperative. “It is true that in the beginning, some men underestimated women and said that the cooperative would not last. But we have learned how to fight back and develop knowledge and skill,” she says.

[6] To promote the cooperative’s success and reputation, Saadia asked its members to organize an exhibition in June 2007, which was a great success. Thanks to her will and courage, this ambitious woman has been able to set an example and give hope to other women in marginalized areas.

Writing

Writing: Should parents limit and control their children’s use of the internet or should they allow them to use it whenever they want? Write a short article to be published in your school magazine expressing your opinion. Give arguments to support your view. (10 pts)

Should Parents Control the Internet?

Nowadays, almost every teenager uses the internet. But should parents limit and control it, or let their children surf whenever they want? In my opinion, parents should set limits.

First of all, the internet contains harmful content, such as violent videos and dangerous websites, which children are not ready to face. Moreover, spending too many hours online makes students lazy and tired, and it affects their studies and their health. Finally, strangers can contact young people through social networks, so some supervision is necessary.

However, control does not mean total prohibition. The internet is also a wonderful tool for research and learning. Parents should talk to their children, agree on reasonable hours and explain the risks.

In conclusion, a balance between freedom and supervision is the best solution.

Exam Tips and Key Vocabulary

What this exam tests

The reading text of the July 2012 Sciences exam is about saadia and the women's artisans' cooperative. The paper tests Reading, Language and Writing, so read the text twice before answering and base every answer on the text.

Key vocabulary from the text

  • cooperative: تعاونية
  • weave: ينسج
  • household: الأسرة
  • leadership: قيادة
  • exhibition: معرض
  • marginalized: مهمّش
  • option: خيار
  • assistance: مساعدة
  • respect: احترام
  • example: مثال

Grammar points in this exam

  • Reported speech
  • Passive voice
  • Third conditional
  • Linking words (whereas, thanks to)

Revise these structures: the Language section of this exam is built on them.

Writing tips

In the writing task you write an article. Give your article a title and open with an introduction that introduces the topic. Develop two or three ideas, one per paragraph, with examples. Use linking words (First of all, Moreover, However, In conclusion) and finish with a short conclusion.
