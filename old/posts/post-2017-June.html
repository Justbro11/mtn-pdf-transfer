[1] A selfie is a self-portrait photo taken with a digital camera or camera phone held in the hand or supported by a stick. Selfies are often posted on social networking services such as Facebook, Instagram, and Twitter. The photo messaging application Snapchat is also largely used to send selfies. The term “selfie” was first used in January 2011 by Jennifer Lee, a lifestyle coach.

[2] Initially popular among young people, selfies have gained wider popularity over time. A survey conducted by Samsung found that they constitute 30% of the photos taken by people aged 18–24. In a society in which images have become a growing means of communication, it has become accepted, and almost expected, for people to post selfies.

[3] Many people send selfies to give a pleasing image of themselves and display their physical portraits. Some users of Snapchat choose to send intentionally unattractive selfies to their friends for comic purposes. For Patricia Wallace, author of The Psychology of the Internet, some people may just use them to share a funny or spontaneous moment with others.

[4] However, according to Zlatan Krizan, a professor of psychology at Iowa State University, people may engage in dangerous activities to take a selfie that attracts attention. In the USA, a 32-year-old woman from North Carolina was driving when her vehicle hit a tree and burst into flames moments after posting selfies online. Luckily, the driver was unharmed. A 21-year-old Spanish man died from electrocution after climbing on top of a train to take a selfie. In Russia, a teenager died while trying to take a selfie on a railway bridge.

[5] Anabel Quan-Haase, a professor at the University of Western Ontario, points out that “as a society, we have moved toward a new type of Net Visual Culture, where we are attracted by pictures of others and descriptions of events. We want to show people what our lives are like. Sometimes, this can be achieved only through taking risks.” Given the number of selfie-related injuries and deaths occurring recently in different countries, it is a must for officials to launch campaigns to warn people of the dangers of taking selfies.

Baccalaureate English National Exam Sciences June 2017: Exam Tips and Key Vocabulary

This Sciences June 2017 exam is built around a reading text about selfies and their effects on young people, followed by language and writing tasks. Use the notes below after you finish the interactive practice, to review what you missed.

What this exam tests

  • Reading (15 pts): comprehension questions, true or false with justification, finding synonyms and identifying references.
  • Language (15 pts): word forms, vocabulary in context, rewriting sentences, language functions and short exchanges.
  • Writing (10 pts): an article on a topic related to the unit.

Key vocabulary from the reading text

  • selfie: صورة ذاتية
  • addiction: إدمان
  • social media: وسائل التواصل الاجتماعي
  • self-esteem: تقدير الذات
  • obsessed: مهووس
  • risky: خطير
  • post: ينشر
  • appearance: مظهر
  • trend: موضة
  • narcissism: حب الذات المفرط

Grammar points to review

  • Passive voice: be + past participle (The exam was corrected by the teachers).
  • Reported speech: shift the tense back and change pronouns and time words (I will come becomes she said she would come).
  • Conditionals: check the type: If + present, will; if + past simple, would; if + past perfect, would have.
  • Linking words: use because of, in order to, as a result, although, so as to correctly.

Writing tips

  • Give your article a title and open with an introduction that presents the topic.
  • Develop two or three ideas, one per paragraph, with examples and linking words (First of all, Moreover, However).
  • Finish with a short conclusion that gives your opinion or advice.
