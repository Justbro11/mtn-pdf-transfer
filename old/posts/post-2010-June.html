[1] In Morocco, each year thousands of young girls from poor families are sent to work in houses in the city. They are often ill-treated and exploited. No official numbers exist, but one non-governmental organization called Dari has counted 23,000 child maids in Casablanca alone.

[2] Halima is one of them. When she was 8 years old, her family sent her to Casablanca to work as a maid. Her father had some financial trouble, so he got in touch with an intermediary whose job was to find maids for rich families. The little girl agreed because she thought that she could be of some help to her parents. But being a maid was not what she really dreamed of. She worked all day long, slept in the kitchen, and was often beaten up by her host family. But she managed to escape from the house where she was working.

[3] Later, someone found her in the street all in tears and helpless, and took her to Dari. Thanks to Dari, Halima now lives at a shelter for former child maids where she is learning cooking and hairdressing. “For the first time since I was 8, I have finally found a place to rest,” Halima said.

[4] Dari’s director says: “The majority of child maids come from the countryside where most families have little money and a lot of kids. If they send a girl away to work as a domestic, that means an important source of income and one less mouth to feed. The parents think that they are doing something nice for their daughters, saving them from the harsh conditions in the countryside, and hoping for a better future for them. But child maids often end up suffering twice: as kids and as adults. When they grow up, they are often exploited by crime gangs.”

[5] Dari’s director further explains that their association works to educate poor parents about the reality of child maids and their exploitation. It also runs programs to sensitize the wealthy families to the rights of their maids: fair treatment, good pay, and basic education.

[6] This issue of child maids is now being addressed by many civil associations. The abuses are being exposed on TV, and more voices are heard against employing child maids, believing that all children should have the right to enjoy their childhood and get a proper education.

Writing: Write an email to your friend telling him/her about what you do in your free time. (Approximately 200 words) (10 pts)

Subject: My free time

Dear Jack,

How are you? I hope everything is fine. You asked me about my free time, so here is my answer.

I don’t have much free time during the week because of my studies, but I try to enjoy myself at the weekend. On Saturday mornings, I usually practice sports: I play football with my friends to keep fit, and it helps me relax after a hard week. In the afternoon, I like listening to music and watching a good film on TV. I also surf the Internet to get information about my hobbies and to chat with friends from other countries, which gives me a chance to practice my English.

On Sundays, I often go out with my friends. We have a wonderful time together, and I think it gives me experience in how to deal with people. Sometimes I play computer games, but not for too long because I don’t want to waste my time.

What about you? What do you do in your free time? Write back soon.

Best wishes,
Yassine

Baccalaureate English National Exam Sciences June 2010: Exam Tips and Key Vocabulary

This Sciences June 2010 exam is built around a reading text about child maids in Morocco, followed by language and writing tasks. Use the notes below after you finish the interactive practice, to review what you missed.

What this exam tests

  • Reading (15 pts): comprehension questions, true or false with justification, finding synonyms and identifying references.
  • Language (15 pts): word forms, vocabulary in context, rewriting sentences, language functions and short exchanges.
  • Writing (10 pts): an email on a topic related to the unit.

Key vocabulary from the reading text

  • maid: خادمة
  • exploit: يستغل
  • shelter: مأوى
  • childhood: الطفولة
  • income: دخل
  • harsh: قاسٍ
  • escape: يهرب
  • sensitize: يحسّس
  • rights: حقوق
  • intermediary: وسيط

Grammar points to review

  • Reported speech: shift the tense back and change pronouns and time words (I will come becomes she said she would come).
  • Third conditional: If + past perfect, would/could have + past participle (talking about an unreal past).
  • Passive voice: be + past participle (The exam was corrected by the teachers).
  • Despite / although: despite + noun or -ing; although + full clause.

Writing tips

  • Start with a subject line and a greeting (Dear ...), then say why you are writing in the first sentence.
  • Develop two or three ideas in short paragraphs, each with a reason or an example, using linking words (First, Moreover, Finally).
  • Finish with a polite closing (Best wishes) and your name.
