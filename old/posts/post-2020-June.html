[1] Greta Thunberg is a climate youth activist who has received worldwide recognition for her efforts to fight climate change. Greta was born on January 3, 2003, in Sweden. Her mother, Malena Ernman, is an opera singer, and her father, Svante, is an actor. She began her climate activism at the age of 15.

[2] In May 2018, Greta won a climate change essay competition in a local newspaper. Three months later, she started protesting in front of the Swedish parliament and skipping classes with the simple message “School strike for climate”. Thanks to social media, her actions have spread rapidly and influenced millions of young people around the world.

[3] The teen activist has been invited to numerous meetings to speak on behalf of the climate youth movement. In December 2018, her speech at the United Nations COP24 in Poland got millions of likes, shares, and views. At the summit, she expressed her deep worry about the environment, saying “I don’t care about being popular. I care about climate justice and the living planet.”

[4] Greta had another opportunity to speak at the UN Climate Action Summit in New York City in September 2019. That is where she walked in the largest climate protest in history with a total of 4 million people.

[5] The world’s eyes were on Greta when she condemned world leaders in her emotional “How Dare You!” speech at the UN, telling them: “You have stolen my dreams and my childhood with your empty words. … People are suffering. People are dying. Entire ecosystems are collapsing… How dare you! We will never forgive you.” In reaction to Greta’s attitude, Anne Hidalgo, the mayor of Paris, said that “It is truly inspiring to see young people making their voices heard.”

[6] In COP25 in Madrid, Greta again blamed leaders for not acting to save the planet because greenhouse gas emissions continue to rise. She realized that her school strikes “haven’t achieved anything”, and that “we need more activists”. For her great efforts to make a difference about climate change, Greta Thunberg was nominated for the Nobel Peace Prize in March 2019.

Writing

Task 1: Complete the following paragraph. (4 pts)

Many people leave the countryside to live in the city for several reasons. First, cities offer more jobs and better salaries, while in the countryside there are few opportunities for young people. Second, schools, universities and hospitals are better and closer in big cities. Third, city life is more exciting because there are cinemas, shops, sports centres and many places to meet friends. Finally, transport and services such as the internet are easier to find in the city. For all these reasons, many people choose to move to urban areas.

Task 2: Write an article for the local newspaper. (6 pts)

Let’s Keep Our Neighborhood Clean!

Our neighborhood is dirty, and this is a problem for all the residents. Rubbish is thrown in the streets, and bad smells and insects make life difficult, so we must find solutions together.

First of all, the municipality should put more bins in the streets and collect the rubbish every day. Moreover, residents should throw their waste in the bins and sort plastic, paper and glass for recycling. Another good idea is to organise cleaning campaigns with the help of local associations and school students. Finally, people who throw rubbish in the street should pay a fine.

If everybody does a small effort, our neighborhood will be cleaner, healthier and more beautiful.

Baccalaureate English National Exam Sciences June 2020: Exam Tips and Key Vocabulary

This Sciences June 2020 exam is built around a reading text about greta Thunberg and young climate activism, followed by language and writing tasks. Use the notes below after you finish the interactive practice, to review what you missed.

What this exam tests

  • Reading (15 pts): comprehension questions, true or false with justification, finding synonyms and identifying references.
  • Language (15 pts): word forms, vocabulary in context, rewriting sentences, language functions and short exchanges.
  • Writing (10 pts): an email on a topic related to the unit.

Key vocabulary from the reading text

  • activist: ناشط
  • climate: المناخ
  • strike: إضراب
  • inspire: يُلهم
  • global warming: الاحتباس الحراري
  • generation: جيل
  • leader: قائد
  • protest: احتجاج
  • awareness: وعي
  • demand: يطالب

Grammar points to review

  • Passive voice: be + past participle (The exam was corrected by the teachers).
  • Reported speech: shift the tense back and change pronouns and time words (I will come becomes she said she would come).
  • Conditionals: check the type: If + present, will; if + past simple, would; if + past perfect, would have.
  • Linking words: use because of, in order to, as a result, although, so as to correctly.

Writing tips

  • Start with a subject line and a greeting (Dear ...), then say why you are writing in the first sentence.
  • Develop two or three ideas in short paragraphs, each with a reason or an example, using linking words (First, Moreover, Finally).
  • Finish with a polite closing (Best wishes) and your name.
