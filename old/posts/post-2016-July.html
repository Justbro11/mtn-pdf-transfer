Text A

[1] Mahatma Gandhi was a great freedom fighter and non-violent activist. He was born in 1869 in Gujarat, India. He completed his schooling in India and went to England for further study of law. He then went to the British colony of South Africa to practice law. There he decided to become a political activist.

[2] Later, he returned to India and started a powerful non-violent movement to fight for India’s independence. Many times, he was sent to prison, but he never discouraged himself and continued fighting for national freedom. He started many mass movements like Non Cooperation Movement in 1920 and the Quit India Movement in 1942.

[3] After so many struggles, India gained independence in 1947. A year later, Gandhi was assassinated. Gandhi proved that everything is possible with non-violence and the unity of people. Even after his death, he is still alive in the heart of every Indian as ‘Father of the Nation’.

Text B

[1] Martin Luther King was a great symbolic leader who worked for equality and civil rights in the United States. He was born in 1929 in Atlanta, Georgia. He was a bright student and skipped grades in both elementary school and high school. He graduated from high school at fifteen. At university, he learned about Mahatma Gandhi who helped change unfair laws in India by teaching people to protest without violence. He thought he could do the same in the USA.

[2] In 1954, Martin organized a protest against racism on buses in Montgomery, Alabama. He began to organize other non-violent protests, and soon was a leader in the civil rights movement. He was arrested and attacked many times. In 1963, he led a march in Washington D.C. with over 200,000 people. In this march, he gave his famous speech, “I have a dream”, in which he said that people should not be judged by the color of their skin. Martin was awarded the Nobel Peace Prize in 1964. Four years later, he was assassinated in Memphis, Tennessee. Martin Luther King’s birthday is a national holiday, and each January his life and work are honored by Americans.

I think the most important invention is the internet. There are at least three reasons why this invention is important in our life. First, it gives us instant access to information, so we can study and learn anything. Second, it connects people all over the world through e-mails and social networks. Third, it has changed work and shopping, because many jobs and services are now online. Finally, it provides entertainment such as videos, music and games.

Task 2: Write an article describing the best city. (6 pts)

Marrakech: The Best City of the Year

If you ask me which city deserves to be the best of the year, my answer is Marrakech.

First of all, it has a fascinating history. Visitors can walk through the old medina and see the Koutoubia Mosque and the Saadian Tombs. Moreover, Jemaa el-Fna Square is full of life: storytellers, musicians and delicious food stalls attract thousands of people every evening. The city also has beautiful gardens such as Majorelle and a warm sunny climate, with the snowy Atlas Mountains nearby. Finally, the people are welcoming and generous.

For all these reasons, Marrakech is a wonder that everybody should visit.

Exam Tips and Key Vocabulary

What this exam tests

The reading text of the July 2016 Sciences exam is about gandhi and Martin Luther King, non-violent leaders. The paper tests Reading, Language and Writing, so read the text twice before answering and base every answer on the text.

Key vocabulary from the text

  • non-violent: سلمي
  • freedom: حرية
  • independence: استقلال
  • assassinate: يغتال
  • protest: احتجاج
  • racism: عنصرية
  • equality: مساواة
  • civil rights: الحقوق المدنية
  • arrest: يعتقل
  • speech: خطاب

Grammar points in this exam

  • Passive voice
  • Reported questions
  • Third conditional
  • Linking words

Revise these structures: the Language section of this exam is built on them.

Writing tips

In the writing task you write an article. Give your article a title and open with an introduction that introduces the topic. Develop two or three ideas, one per paragraph, with examples. Use linking words (First of all, Moreover, However, In conclusion) and finish with a short conclusion.
