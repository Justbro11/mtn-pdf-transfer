[1] Morocco’s street children live a difficult life, often filled with harmful drugs and crime. Efforts to reintegrate them into families and schools are somewhat successful but experts say the problem is large and its root lies in poverty and difficult social circumstances.

[2] Othmane left his home and school at the age of fourteen to live on the street. He no longer wanted to see his mother fight the daily battle to get bread for his five little siblings and pay for his school expenses. “The street is not a better place to live in,” Othmane says, “but at least she will not have to think about my daily living. In the meantime, I may be able to help her.” Othmane carries bags of vegetables and other purchases for customers at a nearby market. In this way, he earns a few dirhams a day, enough to bring a little money back home.

[3] Othmane is one of the growing number of street children in Morocco. These are the homeless and marginalized youths without identity or family. The sidewalks, public gardens, and the buildings’ doorsteps are their beds.

[4] In Casablanca, these children’s main residences are alleys in the old city, the port, the train station, and the fruits and vegetables wholesale market. The port provides them with an opportunity to emigrate illegally. The wholesale market gives them the chance to work as porters. At the train station, they can earn little money from helping passengers or by begging from tourists.

[5] According to the most recent statistics, 7,000 street children live in Casablanca Wilaya alone. 8,800 more live in other major cities such as Marrakesh, Fes, and Meknes. The government and many non-governmental organizations have adopted a strategy of building centers to shelter some of these street children and reintegrate them into schools and families, but the challenge is enormous. The number of children usually exceeds the centers’ capacities; there are not enough beds for all of them, and urgent is the need to act once to save those children.

Writing: Taking drugs is dangerous. Write a short article to your school magazine about the consequences of drug-taking on youths’ health and education. (Approximately 150 words) (10 pts)

Drugs Destroy Young Lives

Taking drugs is one of the most dangerous problems among young people today. Some teenagers try drugs because of curiosity, family problems or pressure from friends.

The effects on health are terrible. Drugs damage the brain and the heart, and they can cause serious diseases and even death. Moreover, drug addicts feel tired, nervous and depressed. Drugs also harm education: young people lose concentration and memory, miss lessons and fail their exams. Some of them finally drop out of school.

What can we do? First, parents and teachers should talk to young people and listen to their problems. Second, schools should organize campaigns to explain the dangers of drugs. Finally, addicts need medical help and support instead of punishment.

In short, the best solution is to say ‘no’ to drugs and to choose sports and studies instead.

Baccalaureate English National Exam Sciences June 2009: Exam Tips and Key Vocabulary

This Sciences June 2009 exam is built around a reading text about street children in Morocco, followed by language and writing tasks. Use the notes below after you finish the interactive practice, to review what you missed.

What this exam tests

  • Reading (15 pts): comprehension questions, true or false with justification, finding synonyms and identifying references.
  • Language (15 pts): word forms, vocabulary in context, rewriting sentences, language functions and short exchanges.
  • Writing (10 pts): an article on a topic related to the unit.

Key vocabulary from the reading text

  • street children: أطفال الشوارع
  • reintegrate: يُعيد إدماج
  • poverty: فقر
  • shelter: يأوي
  • porter: حمّال
  • strategy: استراتيجية
  • homeless: بدون مأوى
  • customer: زبون
  • beg: يتسول
  • capacity: طاقة استيعاب

Grammar points to review

  • Passive voice: modal + be + past participle (Measures should be taken).
  • Reported speech: promise + to + infinitive.
  • Wish clauses: wish + past simple (present) or past perfect (past regret).
  • Concession and purpose: yet / although for contrast; so as to / in order to for purpose.

Writing tips

  • Give your article a title and open with an introduction that presents the topic.
  • Develop two or three ideas, one per paragraph, with examples and linking words (First of all, Moreover, However).
  • Finish with a short conclusion that gives your opinion or advice.
