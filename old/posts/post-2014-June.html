[1] Civil society in Morocco is working hard to fight tobacco and drug addiction among young people. With the help of non-governmental organizations, many teenagers have managed to give up smoking.

[2] Salim, eighteen years old, is one of them. He tells his story to other young people to discourage them from smoking. “A friend offered me my first cigarette. Soon, I started buying my own cigarettes and got into the habit of smoking. As my parents didn’t realize that I was smoking, I carried on until one day a teacher, who was a member of an organization, saved me. He gave me advice and support for several weeks till I finally quitted this bad habit,” Salim said.

[3] Samira, a secondary school student, started smoking and taking drugs when she was 13 because of family problems. “My mother neglected me. She gave me money just to get rid of me. As for my dad, he only came home in the evening and always started shouting at my mother. I easily found refuge in drugs,” she explains.

[4] Amina Baaji, a teacher and member of the Moroccan Association for Listening and Dialogue, has helped several secondary school students put their difficulties behind them. She stresses the importance of the role of parents in dealing with cigarette smoking before it leads to drug addiction. “Families must look after their children and try to understand them so that they will not become addicted to tobacco or drugs,” she says. “Parents’ failure to listen to their children and understand their problems is the main reason why young people start smoking or taking drugs,” she adds.

[5] Jamal Bahaoui, another teacher, agrees with Baaji. “If young people are to be protected from tobacco and drugs, families and schools must keep an eye on them and try to understand their needs and fears,” he explains. “Teachers must spend more time listening to their students so that they can give them guidance and help them deal with their psychological problems,” he adds.

[6] “The best way to get the message across is not by giving advice, but rather by encouraging young people to take part in anti-smoking and anti-drug campaigns,” Jalal Tawfik, a psychiatrist, says. He encourages young people who have overcome addiction to tobacco or drugs to act as an example to others.

Writing: Write a letter to the editor saying whether you agree or disagree with this opinion. Give reasons and examples to support your point of view. (10 pts)

Dear editor,

I am writing in response to your article about teenagers. The article claims that many teenagers do not listen to adults’ advice, do not think about the consequences of their actions and have no sense of responsibility. I partly disagree with this opinion.

First, it is unfair to generalize. Many teenagers listen carefully to their parents and teachers; for example, a lot of students follow their teachers’ guidance and work hard to succeed in their exams. Second, many young people are very responsible. They join NGOs, take part in environmental campaigns and help poor families in their neighborhoods. However, I admit that some teenagers do not think before they act, for instance when they drive too fast or start smoking to imitate their friends.

To sum up, only a minority of teenagers behave irresponsibly. Adults should listen to young people and encourage them instead of criticizing them.

Yours faithfully,
Youssef

Baccalaureate English National Exam Sciences June 2014: Exam Tips and Key Vocabulary

This Sciences June 2014 exam is built around a reading text about teenagers, smoking and drug addiction, followed by language and writing tasks. Use the notes below after you finish the interactive practice, to review what you missed.

What this exam tests

  • Reading (15 pts): comprehension questions, true or false with justification, finding synonyms and identifying references.
  • Language (15 pts): word forms, vocabulary in context, rewriting sentences, language functions and short exchanges.
  • Writing (10 pts): a letter to the editor on a topic related to the unit.

Key vocabulary from the reading text

  • addiction: إدمان
  • quit: يتوقف عن
  • habit: عادة
  • guidance: توجيه
  • neglect: يهمل
  • refuge: ملجأ
  • campaign: حملة
  • psychological: نفسي
  • support: دعم
  • overcome: يتغلب على

Grammar points to review

  • Reported questions: use the statement word order after the question word (Where did you go? becomes he asked where I had gone).
  • Wish clauses: wish + past simple (present) or past perfect (past regret).
  • Relative clauses: who (people), which (things), whose (possession), where (places).
  • Purpose: in order to / so as to + base verb.

Writing tips

  • Start with Dear editor and say clearly in the first paragraph whether you agree or disagree.
  • Support your view with two reasons and an example for each.
  • Finish with a conclusion and a polite closing (Yours faithfully).
