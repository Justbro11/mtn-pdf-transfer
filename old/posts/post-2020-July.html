[1] Tens of millions of animals are killed every year in scientific research and testing. Their number has grown over recent years, most probably because more biomedical research is being carried out around the world. The most commonly used animals are mice, fish, and rats, but many other species including monkeys, cats, dogs, horses, and pigs are also used.

[2] Animals are experimented on for many reasons. These include understanding human biology, developing and testing a cure for humans, and obtaining fundamental knowledge in teaching. Furthermore, many companies depend on animals for testing the safety of their substances and products. Another major reason is developing veterinary treatments for other animals.

[3] Scientists insist that new remedies for serious illnesses cannot be developed unless their safety and efficacy are tested on animals. Similarly, they argue that understanding basic biology and creating new medicines cannot be achieved without animal research. “Consequences will be terrible if research using animals is blocked,” said Nancy Lee, a senior policy adviser in London.

[4] However, we may no longer need to rely on animals to test the safety of different medications, chemicals, or cosmetics in the future. A few months ago, scientists developed the first computer system known as RASCAR which can accurately detect toxic substances. RASCAR is just one of many potential alternatives to animal testing. “I’m extremely optimistic about this and other similar tools to restrict animal testing,” says Andrew Rowan, chief scientific officer for The Humane Society of the United States. “Using animals in tests is significantly very expensive. It takes three years to do comprehensive testing while RASCAR takes only a few minutes,” he adds.

[5] Tom Regan, a philosophy professor in the USA, states: “Animals and people are alike in many ways; they both feel, think, behave, and experience pain.” Thus, animals should be treated with the same respect as humans. When animals are reduced to being mere tools in a scientific experiment, their rights are taken away without any thought of their well-being. Tests often cause pain and suffering or even death.

Writing

Task 1: Complete the following paragraph. (4 pts)

The worst experience I have ever had in my life is losing my phone in a crowded market. It was really a terrible experience because of the following reasons. First, I lost all my photos and contacts, and I could not call my family. Second, I felt very worried and afraid because my personal information was in the phone. Third, I had to spend a lot of money to buy a new one. Finally, I lost many hours looking for it and the police could not find it. I learned to be more careful with my belongings.

Task 2: Write an e-mail to the president of the local council. (6 pts)

Dear Sir or Madam,

I am writing to you about the project for young people in our area. In my opinion, the best choice is the swimming pool.

First of all, swimming is a healthy sport that helps young people stay fit and strong. Moreover, our town gets very hot in summer, and young people have nowhere safe to swim, so many of them go to dangerous places such as rivers. A swimming pool would also create jobs for coaches and lifeguards, and it would bring young people together instead of staying at home with their phones.

I hope the council will consider my suggestion. Thank you for your attention.

Yours faithfully,
Sara

Exam Tips and Key Vocabulary

What this exam tests

The reading text of the July 2020 Sciences exam is about animal testing and its alternatives. The paper tests Reading, Language and Writing, so read the text twice before answering and base every answer on the text.

Key vocabulary from the text

  • experiment: تجربة
  • cruel: قاسٍ
  • laboratory: مختبر
  • alternative: بديل
  • research: بحث
  • cosmetics: مواد التجميل
  • ban: يمنع
  • suffer: يعاني
  • safe: آمن
  • law: قانون

Grammar points in this exam

  • Passive voice
  • Reported speech
  • Conditionals
  • Linking words

Revise these structures: the Language section of this exam is built on them.

Writing tips

In the writing task you write an article. Give your article a title and open with an introduction that introduces the topic. Develop two or three ideas, one per paragraph, with examples. Use linking words (First of all, Moreover, However, In conclusion) and finish with a short conclusion.
